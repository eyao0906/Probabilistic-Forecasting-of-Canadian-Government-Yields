## Research question and accepted baseline design

The project asks whether allowing stochastic volatility in the three Dynamic Nelson–Siegel (DNS) factor innovations improves **joint probabilistic forecasts** of Canadian government yields. The comparison holds fixed the two-step factor extraction, DNS loadings, AR(1) factor means, coefficient priors, prior interpretation for long-run expected factor innovation variance, reconstruction-error treatment, origins and scoring. It changes constant factor innovation variance to a stochastic log-variance process. The revised run changes the mean/persistence and log-variance priors relative to the original baseline; keep their results distinct.

The processed panel has eight maturities (0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 7, 10 and 30 years), 1,356 weekly observations in 2000–2025, and an initial 835-observation training sample ending in 2015. The evaluation uses 130 common expanding-window origins from 2016–2025, every fourth weekly observation, and one- and four-observation-ahead horizons. “Weeks” refer to weekly sample steps, not necessarily exactly seven calendar days.

These are retrospective **observation-time** forecasts: the supplied historical vintage and typical publication delay do not establish which curve was publicly available at each origin. They are not real-time backtests or evidence of executable trading performance.

## Accepted-baseline stochastic-volatility specification and interpretation

For each factor, the stochastic model uses an AR(1) factor mean and a stationary AR(1) process for log innovation variance. The stationary log-volatility variance is denoted by \(v\), with \(q=v(1-\phi^2)\), \(\omega=m-v/2\), and \(h_1\sim N(\omega,v)\). Thus the stationary expected innovation variance conditional on \(m,\phi,v\) is \(\exp(m)\), matching the interpretation of the constant-volatility prior. The transformed persistence prior is \((\phi+1)/2\sim\mathrm{Beta}(20,1.5)\).

In the accepted baseline, the prior is a rate-one exponential for \(v\), truncated to \(0<v<U\), with \(U=4\); representative sensitivities use U=2 and U=8. U bounds stationary **log-volatility variance**, not realized volatility. The bounded prior supports the stated finite posterior-predictive moments at every finite forecast horizon under the proper priors. Do not extend that result to finite unconditional stationary factor variance over the full coefficient-prior mixture, calibrated forecasts, or all-horizon behavior. The baseline uses the wide \(m\sim N(\mu_{0j},25)\) prior, empirically calibrated from the initial training sample and held fixed; it is an empirical-Bayes choice, not independent prior elicitation. The revised candidate instead uses \(m\sim N(\mu_{0j},1)\), while retaining the \(v\) and \(\phi\) priors. The full derivation and caveats are in `docs/bayesian_finite_prior.md`; the revised prior audit is in `docs/bayesian_prior_justification.md`.
