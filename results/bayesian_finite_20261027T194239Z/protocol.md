The variance bound is an assumption in dimensionless log-variance units: stationary log-volatility SD <2 under U=4. At the bound, conditional volatility has multiplicative 95% spread exp(+-1.96) around its median; variance has spread exp(+-3.92). Exponential rate 1 regularizes toward the constant-volatility limit instead of concentrating near the bound. The untruncated rate-1 exponential puts exp(-4) beyond U=4. Initial-window log-squared OLS residual variances (4.8672,5.6714,5.9074), compared with log-chi-square noise variance 4.9348, are consistent with order-one or smaller latent variances; this is a rough check, not a fitted SV estimate. ## Propriety and predictive moments

All component priors are normalized and proper; |phi|<1, 0<v<U, m Gaussian. Conditional on m,phi,v, the full finite latent path has mean m-v/2 and covariance v*phi^|i-j|. Every entry has magnitude <=U. A finite Gaussian factor likelihood is bounded above by (2*pi)^(-n/2)*exp(-sum(h)/2), uniformly in coefficients and observed values. Integrating this bound over Gaussian m and the path is finite uniformly in phi,v: log E exp(-sum(h)/2) <= -n*mu/2+n*U/4+n^2*(25+U)/8. This proves a finite normalizer; positivity of likelihood on proper-prior interior proves a strictly positive normalizer. Bounds are theoretical, evaluated on a log scale, not an implementation using enormous exponentials.

For any fixed future horizon and any finite nonnegative power, future h has the same Gaussian representation and bounded conditional covariance, while m has Gaussian tails. Multiplying the likelihood bound by any finite exponential moment of future h remains integrable (joint Gaussian MGF with uniformly bounded covariance). Coefficient a has Gaussian moments, and |b|<1 bounds finite-horizon coefficient polynomials. Conditional Gaussian innovations therefore give finite posterior-predictive moments of every finite order. This includes second and fourth moments for DSS and moment stability; fixed finite reconstruction noise preserves them. This statement concerns finite horizons, not stationarity of the full prior mixture over factor levels.
